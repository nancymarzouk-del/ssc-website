# WEDDINGS — COLLECTION BLUEPRINT
*A strategic execution plan for the Weddings collection of The Simply Sexy Cigars Journal. Downstream of, and subordinate to, `EDITORIAL-ROADMAP.md` (§7.1, §10, §11) and the frozen `EDITORIAL-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. Planning only — no articles written.*

**Status: DRAFT for Publisher review · uncommitted.** This blueprint proposes the full collection and now includes a **Creative Director** art-direction & editorial-flow pass (§9). The four *new* Companion Features it introduces (marked **NEW**) are recommendations that would be added to the Roadmap's Content Map and Commissioning Queue **on approval** — a meaningful editorial decision, not a restructuring. Nothing here changes production files.

> **One decision to confirm first.** The Roadmap sets the Weddings target at **12**. This blueprint reads that as **12 interconnected touchpoints** — a Feature + 8 home Companions (9 pieces that *live in* Weddings) plus 3 active bridges from Local, Golf, and Education. If instead you want **12 home articles**, we add ~3 more home Companions from the backlog. Recommendation: the 9-home + 3-bridge model — it reaches authority without thinning the collection or cannibalizing Napa/Country-Club/Etiquette, which are stronger in their home collections.

## 1 · COLLECTION MISSION

**Own the wedding-cigar-experience conversation in Northern California — the Journal's highest commercial-intent territory — by making a couple *feel* the hour their guests will remember, and making a planner *trust* who creates it.**

The collection educates first and sells second. It never argues that a wedding *needs* a cigar lounge; it lets the reader arrive there on their own, through story. The brand almost disappears until a single soft invitation at each piece's edge. Success is not a reader who is sold — it is a reader who forwards the piece to the friend who is getting married.

*(Verbatim north star from Roadmap §7.1 — do not re-derive.)*

## 3 · CUSTOMER JOURNEY

*How each persona naturally discovers the Journal and moves through the collection to a soft, single invitation. Entry is usually a search or an AI answer; the path is internal links; the exit is "begin the conversation."*

- **The Bride** — searches *"unique wedding reception ideas"* or asks an engine *"what will guests remember about our wedding?"* → lands on **Beyond the Dance Floor** or the **Feature** → the Feature's Related-Stories lead her to **NorCal Wedding** (she pictures her own venue) → soft invitation at the edge. *Emotional arc: curiosity → recognition → desire → inquiry.*
- **The Groom** — searches *"groomsmen gift ideas"* / *"cigars for the wedding"* → **The Groom's Hour** → up to the **Feature** (why it belongs) → across to **Cigar Etiquette** (he's never hosted cigars before) → invitation.
- **The Wedding Planner** — searches *"how to set up a cigar bar at a wedding"* → **The Coordinator's Guide** (their working document) → **Cigar Etiquette** (to brief nervous couples) → **NorCal Wedding** (venue fit) → a planner-facing invitation to start the conversation about their couples' dates. *This is the collection's primary lead-gen path — planners rebook.*
- **The Venue Coordinator** — searches *"outdoor / country club wedding amenities"* → **NorCal Wedding** or **Country Club Wedding** *(bridge)* → up to the **Feature** → **Coordinator's Guide** (logistics reassurance) → invitation.
- **The Parent of the Bride** — searches *"wedding after party ideas"* / *"how to make the wedding special for guests"* → **Champagne & Cigars — The After-Party** or **The Welcome Evening** → the **Feature** → **Something to Keep** (a favor guests treasure) → invitation.

**Journey principle:** every entry point routes *up* to the Feature (the emotional anchor) and *out* to one sibling that deepens the specific intent — then offers exactly one quiet door to booking. No reader is ever more than two clicks from both the Feature and the invitation.

**Why the order:** Feature (hub) → the two pieces that *convert and multiply* (planner, local) → a cheap seasonal activation → the *reassurance* bridge that de-risks the whole idea → then audience-expanding Companions → then the long tail. This follows **Depth Before Breadth**: each publish strengthens a live Feature and never ships a Companion ahead of its anchor.

## 8 · LEAD-GENERATION STRATEGY  *(educate first, sell second)*

**Where the brand appears.** Nowhere in the middle. Per the Style Guide's two-lens rule, the brand lives only at the **entrance** (an editor's register) and the **exit** (one quiet invitation). The middle is pure story. Simply Sexy Cigars is *named* only near the close of each piece, then hands the reader one door:

- **The one CTA:** "begin the conversation" → `index.html#booking`. One per piece, at the edge, never mid-story, never with a price, never with urgency (Kill List).
- **Tone by audience:** the Feature and couple-facing pieces keep the CTA emotional ("if you're planning a wedding and want an hour your guests will still be describing next year, begin the conversation"). The **Coordinator's Guide** may angle its single invitation to the *planner* ("start the conversation about your couples' dates") — the highest-LTV lead, still soft, still singular.
- **Inspiration blocks, not ad blocks.** Reuse the proven *Beyond the Gift Basket* devices — a "Signature Moment" scene and a restrained "Works Especially Well For" list — as *inspiration a reader could hand back to us*, never as feature lists that narrow the audience. They clear the two-goal test (deepen the reading **and** help the reader picture their own wedding).
- **Subtle CTA placement per piece:** Feature → after the closing scene. Coordinator's Guide → after the checklist. NorCal / Napa / Country Club → after the venue scene, geo-worded. Etiquette → a single reassuring line at the end ("and if you'd like it tended for you…"). Groom's Hour → after the ritual. Others → at the coda.

**The funnel:** the Journal warms (story, trust, zero pressure); the money pages close (booking). A reader arrives at `#booking` already persuaded by feeling. Attribution to watch (Editorial Intelligence §19): planner inquiries from the Coordinator's Guide; couple inquiries from the Feature and NorCal Wedding.

## 9 · ART DIRECTION & EDITORIAL FLOW  *(Creative Director)*

*This section supersedes the earlier uniform "five shot types per article." A magazine does not shoot every story the same way. Each Feature earns its **own** photographic identity, and the collection is paced like an issue — rising, cresting, and settling.*

**Production baseline** (unchanged, Roadmap §16): every image carries the ten-field brief (**Subject · Mood · Time of Day · Lighting · Composition · Lens · Color Palette · Textures · Atmosphere · Editorial Inspiration**). **No logos in frame. No reused imagery between collections** — every Weddings piece needs its own wedding photography. WebP (~q90); hero `fetchpriority="high"`, rest lazy. References: Robb Report / Departures / Vogue Weddings (+ Kinfolk for restraint).

> **Art-direction refinement to confirm.** The house standard (§16) is "warm, cinematic, low-lit." Weddings inverts the emphasis: it **leads in bright daylight luxury and *lands* in the house's signature warmth.** The cigar lounge is the day's *emotional conclusion*, not its visual language. Worth reflecting in the Roadmap on approval — it is how §16 applies to a daytime celebration.

### 9.1 · The Emotional Journey
The collection is built to move a reader through the emotional arc of the wedding weekend. Read in the curated order (§9.6), it rises from quiet anticipation to celebration, deepens into connection, and settles into memory — the same shape as the day itself.

```

   Welcome        Groom's       Beyond the    THE FEATURE   An Autumn   Something
   Evening        Hour ·        Dance Floor   (the heart)   Wedding     to Keep
  (night before)  Coordinator's                 + After-                (the send-off)
                  Guide ·                        Party
                  NorCal Wedding
```

| Emotional beat | The feeling | Feature(s) that carry it |
|---|---|---|
| **Anticipation** | The hush before — the night before, the morning of | The Welcome Evening · The Groom's Hour |
| **Preparation** | The craft and the setting readied | The Coordinator's Guide · A Northern California Wedding |
| **Celebration** | The day at its height | Beyond the Dance Floor |
| **Connection** | Guests gather; conversation becomes the evening | **The Feature** · Champagne & Cigars — After-Party |

*The Feature spans Connection → Reflection → Memory inside itself — which is why it is the issue's centerpiece, not its opening.*

### 9.6 · Magazine Flow  *(does it read as one issue?)*
**Verdict: almost — as pages, strong; as an *issue*, not yet.** Read front-to-back today it would feel like nine excellent but separate features, because there is no curated reading order, the visual language was uniform, and there is no connective "issue" framing. The fixes:

1. **Adopt a curated reading order** — distinct from the commissioning order in §4 (which is sequenced for SEO/business). The *reading* order follows the emotional arc: **The Welcome Evening → The Groom's Hour → The Coordinator's Guide → A Northern California Wedding → Beyond the Dance Floor → The Feature → Champagne & Cigars → An Autumn Wedding → Something to Keep.** Wire it with prev/next (`a-storynav`) so a reader can move through the issue.
2. **Pace the spreads** — the reading order already alternates intimate and cinematic. The one caution: NorCal, Beyond the Dance Floor, and the Feature are three cinematic pieces in a row mid-book — let their signature frames stay visually distinct (landscape / split-frame / the couple) so the crest doesn't blur into one long establishing shot.
3. **Place the Feature as the centerpiece, not the opener** — the classic magazine move: the cover story sits mid-book as the biggest spread, at the emotional peak (Connection). Everything before builds to it; everything after settles down.
4. **Bookend the issue** — open on Anticipation (a quiet, intimate spread) and close on Memory (Something to Keep), so the reader leaves *sitting still*, the way the Style Guide asks every ending to.
5. **Longer-term, give it a cover.** To become *literally* one magazine, the collection eventually wants a `weddings.html` landing page — a cover, an editor's note, and the curated reading order — exactly as the Autumn Edition works. That is a Publisher/architecture decision (Roadmap §8); until then, the carousel card → the Feature, with reading-order prev/next, carries the issue.

*With those five, a reader who moved through all nine pieces in one sitting would feel they had read a single, beautiful wedding issue — bright, rising, and quietly concluded by candlelight.*
